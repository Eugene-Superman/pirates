# The story

What the game tells a player, and how the code tells it. The starting waters' part is built: a prologue, then twelve
steps in five chapters that lead a new captain from the quay to their first ship of a line. The rest of this page is
the plan the later seas will follow.

## The world: the Night the Moon Fell

Forty years ago the Moon cracked and fell into the sea. The tides stopped and the sea broke into **bowls**, walled off
from each other by the **Seams**: walls of everlasting storm. The Moon's shards, **moon glass**, glow on the seabed, and
whatever touches the glass changes: beasts grow into monsters, and men no longer stay drowned.

What the story explains in the game as it is:

| In the game                               | In the story                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Gates to other seas, closed to a low tier | Rifts in the Seams. Only a ship with a strong **keel stone** (a shard in its keel) gets through |
| A gate closed in combat                   | A ship hot from a fight can't hold against the storm                                            |
| Each ship's own levels and talents        | The keel stone drinks the light of the ships it sinks (XP) and grows (talents, signatures)      |
| A sunk ship back at the start             | The Lighthouse's seal: the port's lighthouse burns on a great shard and pulls its captains back |
| The captain's mastery, the account's own  | The captain's own bond with the Lighthouse, whatever ship they sail                             |
| The fog of war                            | The Greyness: without the Moon the nights are dark, and a lookout sees only so far              |
| Sea monsters                              | Beasts that swallowed glass. Pearls from hunts grow round shards inside them                    |

The lines of ships, as the factions they will become: the Royal Navies are the **Admiralty of the Lighthouses** (they
hold the ports and the lights, give the captains their ships, and gather every shard "for safe keeping"); the Empires
of the East the **Mirror Court** (they would grow a new Moon, not mend the old one: hunters of glowing beasts, hence the
harpoons); the Black Flag the **Moonless** (they love the endless night: no tides, no laws); the Frozen Seas the **Ice
Clans** (from the north, where the largest shard froze the sea); the Forbidden Fleets the **Raised** (half-dead ships
lifted off the seabed by shards).

**The turn, for the late seas:** the Moon didn't fall, it was pulled down with chains, on the Night of the Anchor. The
Admiralty wanted lighthouses that never go out, and captains who never die: every time a captain comes back from the
dead, the Moon loses a crumb. The player finds out, step by step, that they have been working for those who stole the
sky. After the Crown Waters the story splits for the online game: the **Tide** (bring the Moon back, break the Seams)
against the **Lighthouse** (keep the world as it is, and the captains' deathlessness): two sides for the guild wars.

Across the seas, in a few lines each: the starting waters (who buys the glass?), the Coral Coast (glowing reefs, glass
on the turtles' shells), the Straits and the Windward Isles (the black market of glass, the first of the Moonless; the
thread leads to the Admiralty's stores), the Fog Banks and the Shipwreck Shoals (where the Moon struck first: the first
Raised), the Storms, the Kraken's Deep and the Black Reefs (the Seams at their closest; the kraken swallowed a shard
the size of an island), the Iron Sea, the Ember Isles and the Serpent's Maw (the Admiralty's forges, where glass is
melted into keel stones, and the records that show the Moon didn't fall by itself), the Ice, the Abyss and the Crown
Waters (the greatest shard in the north, a heart beating in the Abyss, and the Crown itself: the biggest shard of all,
and the Lord Anchor, who pulled the chain).

## The people of the starting waters

| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Edda**                  | The lighthouse keeper of Saltwind. Warm, knows the truth, and the first to lie to the player          | Painted: auburn hair greying, a green shawl, a lantern, her keys                   |
| **Tobin**                 | The bosun: the voice at sea, who teaches the controls                                                 | Painted: grey-bearded, a sailor's cap, a coil of rope, a whistle                   |
| **Hale**                  | The harbour master: pays for the dues, posts the tavern's bounties                                    | Painted: a grey moustache, a claret coat with anchor buttons, a ledger, keys       |

Edda calls the captain "you" as an old friend would (Ukrainian and Russian: _ти_, _ты_); Tobin and Hale are formal
(_ви_, _вы_). Nothing said of the captain is gendered in any language: the portraits are of men and women.

## The starting waters: "Who buys the glass?"

The prologue first, four cards: the Moon falls; the Seams rise; the glass changes what it touches, and the
Lighthouse's captains no longer stay drowned; the player was one of the drowned, and this morning the sea gave them
back. Then the player picks their captain's face, and wakes in port, where Edda is waiting.

| #   | Chapter            | Step                  | What to do                                     | Turned in | Teaches                      |
| --- | ------------------ | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | --------- | ---------------------------- |
| 1   | The Returned       | Fitting out           | Fit 4 cannons and 4 harpoon guns from the hold | at once   | The loadout (L)              |
| 2   |                    | First course          | Sail to the buoy off the roadstead             | at once   | The helm: a click on the sea |
| 3   |                    | Not fish              | Sink 2 fishing boats, haul up the moon glass   | at once   | Firing (Q, Space), loot      |
| 4   |                    | Just glass            | Sail into the harbour                          | in port   | The port                     |
| 5   | The price of glass | The shard wakes       | Reach level 2, take a talent                   | at once   | Levels, talents (K)          |
| 6   |                    | The dues              | Sink 2 merchant sloops, sell goods             | at once   | The market                   |
| 7   | Glass in the deep  | Glass in the shells   | Take the harpoon guns, hunt 2 sea turtles      | at once   | Harpoons (E), monsters       |
| 8   |                    | What drags them under | Kill 2 sea serpents, repair at sea             | at once   | The repair (R)               |
| 9   | The Baron          | The Baron's men       | Sink 3 smuggler sloops                         | in port   | Hostile bots                 |
| 10  |                    | Scouting              | Scout the Baron's lair                         | at once   | The sea chart (M)            |
| 11  |                    | The Smuggler Baron    | Sink the Baron                                 | in port   | A boss and his mortar        |
| 12  | The Seam's seal    | The Seam's seal       | Buy a tier 1 ship at the shipyard              | at once   | The shipyard, the gates      |

What happens: the sloop's guns are in its hold, and Edda has the player fit them before they sail (with a few hundred
of the plainest cannonballs and harpoons aboard, nothing else); then she sends them out to the buoy. The fishing boats
on the reefs drag their nets for **glass**, not fish: the player brings a shard of **moon glass** back (a good in the
hold, which every one of the story's prey leaves), and Edda takes it, says it is "just glass" (her first lie) and tells
of the Fall and the Seams. The shard in the keel wakes at level 2 ("...the sky... I remember the sky..."). Hale, the harbour master, has
the merchants who slip past their dues sunk, and notices they carry glass, all of it bound north-east. The turtles grow
glass in their shells, the serpents have eaten it. A smuggler's papers name the buyer: the Baron. The player scouts his
lair (the bosun's spyglass on him: the camera turns to him through the fog, and nothing falls on the ship meanwhile),
then sinks him; aboard are crates of glass and the **seal of a Seam**. Edda takes his papers ("the harbour's
business now"; the shard: "...she knows who paid him..."). The seal opens a Seam, but the sloop's shard is too small to
pass one: the shipyard lays a stronger keel stone in a new hull, **under any flag**. With the sloop at its last level
and the gold saved, the player buys a tier 1 ship of the line they choose, and the Seam to the east lets them through
to the Coral Coast.

The lines themselves are in [src/game/story/story.ts](../src/game/story/story.ts) (`STORY`, `PROLOGUE`), English, and
in `uk.ts` and `ru.ts` translated: change them there.

The tavern's bounties stay beside the story, for gold and experience between its steps, taken again and again.

## How the code tells it

- `game/story/story.ts` holds it all as data: the speakers, the prologue's cards, the chapters, and each step's
  objectives (`reach` a mark, `sink` so many of a kind, `fit` so many guns, `haul` loot or a good, `dock`, `level`,
  `talent`, `repair`, `sell`, a `weapon` picked, a `ship` of a tier), its opening and closing lines, how it is turned
  in, its reward, a good it takes from the hold, and a kind the crew watches while its closing is told.
- A player's progress (`player.story`, saved): the prologue seen, the step they are on, whether its opening has been
  told, each objective's count, and a step done whose closing is still to be told. Objectives count only once the
  opening has been told. A step turned in `'done'` ends the moment its objectives are met, its closing told wherever
  the ship is; one turned in `'port'` waits till the ship docks, and its closing is told there.
- `stepStory` (in `Game.update`, before the stats) counts the update's events and the ship towards the objectives.
  `storyTalk` says what is to be told now; the client plays it and calls `tellStory` with its key when it was heard
  out (or skipped): that, and `seePrologue`, are the player's actions, commands to the server online.
- A conversation never pauses the game: online the sea can't stop for one player.
- The first step keeps the ship in port while its guns wait in the hold (`storyFitting`, `setSail`): Set sail is
  dimmed under a note, and the player's ship at the quay, a medallion over the sloop on the harbour's picture, calls
  them to it with a "!" (the minimap's loadout button wears one too). It opens the loadout, where a guide shows how a
  gun is fitted: the game's gold pointer carrying a cannon from the hold onto the empty slot lit for it, again and
  again, a note over it; the cannons fitted, the harpoon guns' tab is lit, and the same for them. Never a player shut
  in: with no guns to fit the ship sails.
- The story's places (`StoryMark`: the buoy, the spot off the Baron's lair) are placed on open water when the sea is
  made, like the gates, and no bot comes into the game or wanders near them; while a step asks the player home (or
  to sell the goods aboard, once that is all it still asks), its mark is the harbour. Where the story sends the player (`storyGoal`: that mark, or the nearest of its prey) is always
  on the minimap in breathing gold, a painted marker (a star, the harbour's lighthouse, the prey's crescent), on its rim
  the way to steer while it is far off, and on the sea chart with every one of the prey; the bosun says so as the first
  course and the fishing boats begin. The mark glows gold in the water, rings of light spreading from it, bent by the waves, a
  pillar of soft gold light over it with gold motes rising (the harbour's taller, over the town); on the sea it has its
  buoy, and
  a painted gold arrow round the ship points to it while it is off the screen, or to the prey while the fog hides it.
  On the sea chart a gold arrow hangs over it; on the world chart over the sea the player is in.
- What a step asks the player to sink is marked gold (a painted gold arrow over it, a ring round it on the water, a gold
  crescent on the minimap and the sea chart), and the spawner brings it in near them, out of sight but close, weaker than its kind, each leaving a shard
  of moon glass: the story's prey (bots/spawner.ts, `stepPrey`), never a boss. Online each player's own.
- The Baron scouted (`watch` on the step): while its closing is told, the ship sees him through the fog, the camera
  turns to him and back after, and no bot starts a fight with the ship (`Ship.spotted`, `Ship.parley`).
- A player who reads none of it still gets through: whatever the step asks to be pressed is lit
  (`client/hud/storyCue.ts`, `StoryGuide.ts`), a gold light breathing round it, the game's gold pointer clicking it
  with rings of light going out, and a short note. In port Set sail while the step is done at sea, the market's
  medallion while there are goods to sell, then its Sell tab and Sell everything, and the shipyard's medallion and the
  new ship's Buy once its terms are met; at sea the talents' button once a point waits for the step's talent (in the
  tree the talent to take spotlit, all else dimmed, and Yes in the question), the weapon to pick and the repair once the
  hull can be mended. Nothing while a conversation is told.
- The bosun's tips (`client/hud/Tips.ts`): the first three times the player fires cannonballs at a beast or harpoons at
  a hull, fires with no guns or ammo at hand, runs out of or low on ammo, or fills the hold, a line of the bosun's in
  the conversation box, going by itself after a while; never while the story speaks. The browser keeps how often each
  was given.
- The goal it leads to is the shipyard's: `economy/shipyard.ts`, the first ship after the starter sloop of tier 1 and
  any line (`TIER_TERMS`, the story told up to the Seam's seal), each next one the next of its line.
- An older save, from before the story, is told to its end if the player was past the starting waters' first steps
  (a tier 1 ship, five tavern quests, the Baron sunk); else the story starts for them, the prologue seen.
- The settings' Debug group has **Story: Skip step**, to test a later step without playing the ones before it; New game
  shows a new player's first minute again.

Adding a step: its id in `StoryStepId`, the step in `STORY` where it goes (a save keeps the step by id), its texts in
`uk.ts` and `ru.ts`. A new kind of objective: in `Objective`, and how it is counted in `progress`.

## Pictures

The painted ones are in the art's folder (`blender-assets/story/source/`, outside this repo), made into
`public/sprites/story/` by `story/build_story_art.py` there: a picture repainted, run it again.

- **The prologue's four pictures** (`client/hud/Prologue.ts`), painted: `prologue_1.webp` to `prologue_4.webp`, cut
  to 16:9 and 1120 × 630 pixels (shown 560 × 315 at most):
  1. The Moon falling into the sea, the sky cracked behind it.
  2. A Seam: a wall of storm across the sea, a ship small before it.
  3. Moon glass glowing on the seabed, and a sea serpent over it.
  4. Saltwind Harbour, its lighthouse lit, a sloop at the quay.
- **The speakers' portraits** (`client/hud/DialogueBox.ts`), round, 104 pixels across on the screen, all four painted:
  each bust (on a clear ground) cut square round the face (the script's `FACES`), the voice in the keel's round its
  shard, into `speakers.webp`, 256 pixels. A speaker to come is painted the same way (a bust, the face in the middle);
  until then, and while the portraits load, their initial in a gold ring stands in (`COLORS` its tint).
- **The story's buoy on the water** (`client/scene/models/storyMark3d.ts`: a vector buoy with a lamp now), if it is to
  be modelled like the ships.
